Ukraine Barometer · Issue No. 2 · 17–23 November 2026

This week's key points

Resilience index
2.3 / 5
  • Energy: strikes on substations continue, the south and east are going dark

    Since September Russia has struck the energy system more than 1,000 times, mostly high-voltage substations (Energy Ministry). New outages hit 8 oblasts on 18 November and emergency cuts 5 oblasts on 22 November; Odesa's grid was damaged. NASA imagery shows national night brightness 9.5% below August, with Zaporizhzhia and Odesa oblasts darkest (about −30%).

  • About 60 paid-for Patriot missiles await Pentagon approval

    According to the Financial Times, PAC-3 and PAC-2 interceptors funded by allies have still not been handed over because the US defence secretary has not signed off. The president reported talks with specific countries to speed up air-defence deliveries.

  • The EU formally tabled a €45bn loan for 2027

    On 18 November the Commission proposed a loan Ukraine repays only once it receives Russian reparations, with interest subsidised by EU states. Germany's chancellor threatened to veto the EU budget if all 27 do not back it. Leaders hold a first debate at the next summit.

  • Inflation confirmed: 11% in October

    The statistics service confirmed annual inflation rose to 11% (10% in September), the highest since November 2024. The NBU's next rate decision is on 17 December; the hryvnia firmed slightly over the week.

  • The anti-corruption strategy again failed to reach a vote

    As of 19 November bill No. 15230-d had not passed even a first reading, and the government is preparing its own version. In the Carthage case, investigators say SAPO has documented over UAH 1bn in illicit benefit and charged a former head of a Prosecutor General's Office department.
